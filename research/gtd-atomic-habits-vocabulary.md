# The GTD and Atomic Habits source vocabulary, as glossary terms

Research for [#16](https://github.com/FDavalosD/canoli/issues/16). Parent map: [#1](https://github.com/FDavalosD/canoli/issues/1). Feeds [#8](https://github.com/FDavalosD/canoli/issues/8) (item types).

This builds on two earlier findings files: `research/gtd-enforceable-rules.md` (branch `research/gtd-enforceable-rules`, #2) and `research/atomic-habits-enforceable-mechanics.md` (branch `research/atomic-habits-enforceable-mechanics`, #3). Those two cover *enforcement*. This file covers the *terms*: what each word means in its source, how it relates to its neighbours, and where the two books use the same word for different things. Existing project terms (Household, Member, Proof, Escalation, Capture) follow [GLOSSARY.md](../GLOSSARY.md).

## Answer in one line

GTD gives Canoli its vocabulary for **one-off commitments and the lists that hold them** (Inbox, Next Action, Project, Waiting For, Someday/Maybe, Calendar, Tickler, Context, Weekly Review, Horizons). *Atomic Habits* gives it the vocabulary for **behaviour that repeats** (Habit, Cue, Implementation Intention, Habit Stack, Gateway Habit, Habit Tracker, Never Miss Twice, Habit Contract). The sources share a few words with different meanings, so the glossary needs separate names for them: the **two "two-minute rules"** (GTD: do a quick action now; Clear: shrink a new habit to a two-minute starter), **"goal"** (a GTD horizon vs. what Clear says to de-emphasise in favour of systems), and **"review"** (GTD's Weekly Review vs. Clear's annual review and integrity report). Neither source defines **"chore"** or **"routine"** as a technical term. GTD can only hold recurring work as repeating calendar entries, tickler items or checklists, and Clear treats any regular behaviour as a habit. So the Chore/Routine question is still open for #8.

## GTD terms

### The five steps

GTD describes workflow as five steps: Capture, Clarify, Organize, Reflect, Engage [GTD Ch. 2; WHAT]. The 2001 edition called them Collect, Process, Organize, Review, Do [GTD-2001 Ch. 2]. The 2015 edition renamed them, but they are the same steps. Canoli should use the 2015 names.

| Term | Source definition (paraphrased) | Relations |
|---|---|---|
| **Stuff** | Anything that has come into your physical or mental world and doesn't belong where it is, because you haven't yet decided what it means: its desired outcome and its next action [GTD Ch. 2]. | The raw material of **Capture**. It stops being "stuff" once it is clarified. |
| **In-tray / Inbox** (Allen also says "in-basket" and "collection tool") | A place outside your head where stuff waits to be clarified. Keep as few as you can, and empty every one regularly [GTD Ch. 2, 5]. "Empty" means each item has been decided, not necessarily done [GTD Ch. 6]. | Filled by **Capture** and emptied by **Clarify**. Canoli's GLOSSARY already speaks of "a Member's GTD inbox". |
| **Capture** | Collecting whatever has your attention into an in-tray [GTD Ch. 2, 5; WHAT]. | The first step. Project GLOSSARY: Capture never decides meaning. |
| **Clarify** (2001: Process) | Deciding, for each captured item, what it is and whether it is actionable. If it is, decide the next action and whether a project is involved [GTD Ch. 2, 6]. Rules: top item first, one item at a time, nothing goes back into the in-tray [GTD Ch. 6]. | Takes an item from the **Inbox**. The outcomes are trash, **Someday/Maybe**, **Tickler**, **Reference**, a **Two-Minute Action** done now, **Waiting For** (delegate), **Calendar**, or a **Next Actions** list, plus a **Project** if more than one step is needed. |
| **Organize** | Putting the results of clarifying into the right buckets: the lists, the calendar and the reference files [GTD Ch. 2, 7]. | Its output is the set of lists below. |
| **Reflect** (2001: Review) | Looking over the system often enough to keep it trusted and current. Daily you look at the calendar and action lists. Weekly you do the Weekly Review [GTD Ch. 2, 8]. | Its keystone is the **Weekly Review**. Higher **Horizons** get reviewed less often. |
| **Engage** (2001: Do) | Choosing what to do in the moment, using the system. Filter by context, then time available, then energy, then priority [GTD Ch. 2, 9]. | Draws on the **Next Actions** lists sorted by **Context**. |

### Things on lists

| Term | Source definition (paraphrased) | Relations |
|---|---|---|
| **Next Action** | The next physical, visible activity that moves something forward, such as a call, an errand or a draft [GTD Ch. 2, 6; PROJ]. | Lives on a Next Actions list by **Context**. Every active **Project** needs at least one [GTD Ch. 8; WR]. Not dated: dated actions go on the **Calendar**. |
| **Project** | Any desired outcome that needs more than one action step and can be finished within about a year [GTD Ch. 2; PROJ]. You don't *do* a project. You do its actions, and the project list reminds you of the open loop [GTD Ch. 2]. | H1 of the **Horizons of Focus**. It has **Project Support** material and at least one **Next Action**, **Waiting For** or **Calendar** entry. It sits under an **Area of Focus**. Planned with **Natural Planning** when needed. |
| **Project Support material** | Notes, plans and other material for an active project. It is not itself a reminder of an action [GTD Ch. 7]. | Kept apart from the Projects list and from **Reference**. Allen distinguishes them: support material is for an *active* project, reference is general. |
| **Waiting For** | A list of things you have delegated or are expecting from someone else, ideally with the date you asked [GTD Ch. 6, 7]. | The **Clarify** outcome "delegate". It is reviewed weekly [WR]. Allen also lists it as a context [CTX]. |
| **Someday/Maybe** | Things you might want to do but are not committed to now. They are parked and reviewed weekly so they can be activated or dropped [GTD Ch. 2, 7; WR]. | A non-actionable outcome of **Clarify** (incubate). Never on action lists. |
| **Reference** | Non-actionable information worth keeping, filed so it can be found later [GTD Ch. 2, 7]. | Another non-actionable outcome. It carries no action and no date. |
| **Calendar (the "hard landscape")** | Only things tied to a day or time: time-specific actions, day-specific actions, and day-specific information. Wishes and would-like-tos don't go there [GTD Ch. 7]. | The "Ground" of the Horizons, together with Next Actions [HOF]. Recurring dated commitments can repeat on it. |
| **Tickler** | A system, traditionally 43 folders (31 daily plus 12 monthly), that brings items back to your attention on a chosen future date. It is how you "mail things to yourself" in the future [GTD Ch. 7]. | It holds *reminders to reconsider* or deferred material, not appointments. The calendar can do the same job. This is GTD's main home for things that come back periodically. |
| **Context** | What an action needs in order to be done: a tool, a place or a person (@Calls, @Home, @Errands, @Computer, @Agendas, @Anywhere). Allen calls it the first filter on what you can do right now [GTD Ch. 7; CTX]. | It sorts **Next Actions**. *It is not a trigger* (compare Clear's **Cue** below). |
| **Agenda** | A list per person (or per meeting) of things to raise the next time you meet [GTD Ch. 7; CTX]. | A special kind of **Context**. In the Household, one per Member about the other. |
| **Checklist** | A reminder list for recurring or standard tasks, or for keeping an area in order, such as a travel packing list or the Weekly Review checklist itself [GTD Ch. 7]. | This is where Allen puts repeatable *routines*. It is not part of the next-action flow. See "No clean equivalent" below. |

### Review and planning

| Term | Source definition (paraphrased) | Relations |
|---|---|---|
| **Weekly Review** | A weekly session in three phases. Get Clear: collect loose papers, process to zero, mind sweep. Get Current: review action lists, past and upcoming calendar, Waiting For, Projects and checklists. Get Creative: review Someday/Maybe and generate new ideas [WR; GTD Ch. 8]. | The keystone of **Reflect**. It includes a **Mind Sweep**. |
| **Mind Sweep** | A deliberate brain-dump, often prompted by a trigger list (home, family, money, health…), to capture every open loop still in your head [GTD Ch. 5; WR]. | A form of **Capture**, done at the start and in every Weekly Review. |
| **Horizons of Focus** | Six levels of commitment, from the ground up: **Ground**: calendar and actions; **H1** Projects; **H2** Areas of Focus and Accountability; **H3** Goals and Objectives (about 1–2 years); **H4** Vision (about 3–5 years); **H5** Purpose and Principles. Priority flows top-down [HOF; GTD Ch. 2]. The 2001 edition used altitude names: Runway (current actions), 10,000 ft (current projects), 20,000 ft (areas of responsibility), 30,000 ft (1–2 year goals), 40,000 ft (3–5 year vision), 50,000+ ft (life purpose) [GTD-2001 Ch. 2]. | Use the H-numbers. "Runway" is the old name for Ground. |
| **Area of Focus** (also "Area of Responsibility/Accountability") | An ongoing sphere of life or work that you keep up to a standard, with no end state (health, home, finances, family, self-development) [GTD Ch. 2; HOF]. | H2. It spawns **Projects** and Next Actions when something falls short of the standard. Unlike a Project, it is never "done". |
| **Goal** (GTD sense) | H3: an outcome you want within about 1–2 years [HOF; GTD Ch. 2]. | Above Areas, below Vision. |
| **Two-Minute Rule** (GTD) | While clarifying, if an action takes less than two minutes, do it now, because tracking it would cost more than doing it. Two minutes is a guideline [GTD Ch. 6; 2MR]. | Applies to *one-off* items in **Clarify**. It has nothing to do with habit formation. |
| **Natural Planning Model** | How the mind naturally plans an outcome, in five phases: purpose and principles, outcome or vision, brainstorming, organizing, and identifying next actions [GTD Ch. 3]. | Used on **Projects** that need more than a list of steps. Most projects need only the last phase [GTD Ch. 3]. |

## Atomic Habits terms

| Term | Source definition (paraphrased) | Relations |
|---|---|---|
| **Habit** | A routine or behaviour done regularly, in many cases automatically [AH Ch. 1]. Clear explains it as the four-stage loop below, which over time runs with little conscious thought [SUM; AH Ch. 3]. | The core unit. Built with the **Four Laws**. It is the *repeated behaviour*, not the outcome. Note that Clear's own definition uses "routine" as a near-synonym. |
| **Habit Loop** | The four stages of every habit [AH Ch. 3; SUM]. **Cue**: the trigger that predicts a reward. **Craving**: the motivation to change state. **Response**: the actual behaviour, which is the habit itself. **Reward**: the payoff that satisfies the craving and teaches the brain to repeat the loop. Cue and craving form the problem phase; response and reward form the solution phase [AH Ch. 3]. | Each stage has a **Law**. |
| **Four Laws of Behavior Change** and **inversions** | To build a habit: 1 make it obvious (cue), 2 attractive (craving), 3 easy (response), 4 satisfying (reward). To break one: 1 invisible, 2 unattractive, 3 difficult, 4 unsatisfying [AH Ch. 3; SUM]. | The framework that organises every mechanic below. |
| **Identity-based habits** | Change happens at three layers: outcomes (what you get), processes (what you do), identity (what you believe about yourself). Lasting change starts from identity: decide who you want to be, then prove it with small wins. Each repetition is a "vote" for that identity [AH Ch. 2; SUM]. | Gives habits their *why*. The nearest GTD equivalents are H4/H5 (Vision, Purpose) and H2 (Areas). |
| **Systems vs Goals** | Goals are the results you want; systems are the processes that lead to them. Clear argues you should focus on systems, because people with the same goal differ in their systems, and achieving a goal is only a moment [AH Ch. 1; SUM]. | A **Habit** is a piece of a system. See the clash with GTD **Project** and **Goal** below. |
| **Plateau of Latent Potential** | Habits compound, but results often stay invisible for a long time before a breakthrough. Early work isn't wasted, it's stored [AH Ch. 1]. | The reason to keep going through the early phase of **Habit Tracking** and **Never Miss Twice**. |
| **Habits Scorecard** | An awareness exercise: list your current daily behaviours and mark each one good (+), bad (−) or neutral (=) [AH Ch. 4; RES]. | Comes before the First Law. |
| **Implementation Intention** | A plan stating when and where you will act: "I will [behaviour] at [time] in [location]" [AH Ch. 5; II]. | A way to make the **Cue** obvious (Law 1). A **Habit Stack** is a variant that uses another habit as the cue. |
| **Habit Stacking** | Anchoring a new habit to an existing one: "After [current habit], I will [new habit]." The anchor should be reliable, as frequent as the new habit, and specific [AH Ch. 5; HS]. | A special **Implementation Intention**. Stacks can chain. |
| **Environment design** | Shaping your surroundings so good cues are visible and bad cues are hidden. One space, one use [AH Ch. 6–7; ENV]. | Laws 1 and inverted 1. Self-control is mostly avoiding temptation, not resisting it [AH Ch. 7]. |
| **Temptation bundling** | Pairing something you need to do with something you want to do. It can be combined with stacking [AH Ch. 8; TB]. | Law 2. |
| **Two-Minute Rule** (Clear) / **gateway habit** | When starting a new habit, shrink it to a version that takes under two minutes (e.g. "put on running shoes"). The point is to master showing up; "standardize before you optimize." Clear calls the small first step a gateway habit [AH Ch. 13; 2MIN]. | Law 3. It is about *ramping up a repeating behaviour*, the opposite job from GTD's rule. |
| **Habit Tracking** / **Habit Tracker** | Recording each time you do a habit, for example by crossing off a calendar ("don't break the chain"). Tracking is obvious, attractive and satisfying at once. Track right away and only a few key habits [AH Ch. 16; HT]. | Law 4. Canoli's **Proof** is its tracking. |
| **Never Miss Twice** | Missing once is an accident; missing twice is the start of a new habit. Get back on track at once [AH Ch. 16; HT]. | A rule about the **Habit Tracker**. #3 uses it as the threshold for **Escalation**. |
| **Accountability partner** | Someone who watches your behaviour, so that skipping has an immediate social cost [AH Ch. 17]. | Inverted Law 4. In the Household this is the other **Member**. |
| **Habit Contract** | A written, signed agreement stating the habit and the penalty for breaking it, also signed by one or two partners [AH Ch. 17; RES]. | It makes the accountability partner formal. |
| **Goldilocks Rule** | Motivation is highest when a task is at the edge of current ability, neither too easy nor too hard [AH Ch. 19; GL]. | Decides when a **gateway habit** should grow. |
| **Annual Review / Integrity Report** | Clear's own reflection practice: a yearly look at habits and progress, and a mid-year check on whether you are living by your values [AH Ch. 20]. | The habit-side counterpart of GTD's **Weekly Review** and Horizons reviews (see clashes). |

## Where the two systems overlap or clash

1. **Two different "two-minute rules".** GTD's rule is about *triage*: while clarifying, a one-off action under two minutes gets done right away instead of listed [GTD Ch. 6; 2MR]. Clear's rule is about *design*: a new repeating behaviour gets scaled down to a two-minute starter version [AH Ch. 13]. They have different inputs (an inbox item vs. a new habit), different moments (clarifying vs. creating a habit) and different outputs (done and gone vs. a small habit that grows). **Use distinct names: "Two-Minute Action" (GTD) and "Gateway Habit" (Clear). Don't use "two-minute rule" without saying which.**
2. **GTD recurring items vs Clear's habits.** GTD has no "habit" object. Recurring things live as repeating **Calendar** entries (when the day matters), **Tickler** items (bring this back on a date), or **Checklists** (routines you run through) [GTD Ch. 7]. Allen's whole model assumes an action gets *done once and leaves*. Clear's model assumes a behaviour *repeats with no end*, and success is measured by streaks and identity [AH Ch. 1–2, 16]. So "water the plants every Sunday" can be either a GTD recurring calendar item or a Clear habit, depending on which promise you make: *it will be done* (GTD) or *I'm someone who does this* (Clear). This is the core of #8.
3. **GTD Project vs Clear's goals and systems.** A GTD Project is a finishable outcome with a list of actions [GTD Ch. 2]. Clear's "goal" is also an outcome, but he tells you to put the system first [AH Ch. 1]. They don't actually contradict each other: GTD also says you can't *do* a project, only its actions. The difference is that GTD *tracks* outcomes as first-class items, while Clear plays them down. A Habit can serve a Project or an Area (e.g. "run 3×/week" serves "Run a 10k in March"), but it isn't a Project's Next Action, because it never finishes.
4. **Two meanings of "goal".** In GTD, "goal" is a horizon (H3, 1–2 years) [HOF]. In Clear, "goal" is the general idea of a desired result, contrasted with systems [AH Ch. 1]. They are close enough to share one word, but the glossary should define it the GTD way (H3) and treat Clear's point as advice about goals, not a different object.
5. **Horizons / Areas of Focus vs Identity.** H2 Areas of Focus ("Health", "Home") are standards you keep up. H4/H5 (Vision, Purpose and Principles) say who you want to be and why. Clear's identity ("I am a runner", "we keep a tidy home") falls somewhere between: it is expressed as identity, like H5, but held in place by repeated behaviour, like H2 standards. A reasonable mapping is that an identity belongs to an Area of Focus, and habits are its votes. Neither source draws this link. It is an inference.
6. **Weekly Review vs habit review.** GTD's Weekly Review is about *commitments*: are the lists complete, does every project have a next action [WR]. Clear's reviews (Annual Review, Integrity Report) are yearly and about *identity and progress*, and the Goldilocks check is ongoing [AH Ch. 19–20]. Clear gives no weekly ritual. The overlap is clear: habits could be reviewed in the Get Current step of the Weekly Review. That is a design choice, not something either source says.
7. **Context vs Cue.** Both involve place. GTD's Context is a *filter*: you are @Home, so these actions are possible [GTD Ch. 7; CTX]. Clear's Cue is a *trigger*: arriving home starts the behaviour [AH Ch. 3, 5]. Context answers "what can I do here?", and Cue answers "what do I do when this happens?". They should stay separate terms.
8. **Mind Sweep vs Habits Scorecard.** Both are awareness inventories. The Mind Sweep lists *open loops* (commitments), and the Scorecard lists *current behaviours* and rates them. They complement each other.
9. **Waiting For / Agenda vs Accountability partner / Habit Contract.** Both systems use other people, in opposite directions. GTD tracks what *others* owe *you* (Waiting For) and what you need to *raise* with them (Agenda). Clear uses others to watch *you* (partner, contract). In the Household, each Member plays all of these roles for the other.
10. **Next Action vs Response.** The GTD Next Action is a one-off physical step. Clear's Response is the habit's behaviour, repeated. Both stress concrete, physical verbs, and GTD's "next physical visible action" test is a good way to word a habit's behaviour as well.

## No clean equivalent

- **Chore.** Neither book defines it as a term. Allen would treat a household chore as a Next Action (one-off), a recurring Calendar or Tickler item (dated repeats), or a Checklist item (a routine run-through), and the standard it serves as an Area of Focus ("Home") [GTD Ch. 2, 7]. Clear uses everyday chores (doing the dishes, laundry, tidying) as examples of *habits* [AH Ch. 5, 12–13], with no special category. **Still open: #8 decides what a Chore is in Canoli.**
- **Routine.** Allen uses it in everyday language, with Checklists as the place to keep routines [GTD Ch. 7]. Clear uses "routine" as a near-synonym for habit, and for a sequence of habits (a stack, a warm-up or "motivation ritual") [AH Ch. 1, 5, 10]. Neither source gives it a technical meaning. **Still open for #8.**
- **Streak.** This is Clear's everyday word for an unbroken chain [AH Ch. 16; HT]. It isn't a formal term, and GTD has no counterpart.
- **Bad habit.** Clear's inversions cover breaking habits. GTD has nothing equivalent, since you can't "stop doing" something with a next action.
- **Daily review.** Allen mentions checking the calendar and action lists daily [GTD Ch. 8], but gives it no structured procedure the way he does the Weekly Review.

## Proposed glossary entries

Opinionated. Each entry says what the thing *is*. Canoli's own item types (Chore, Routine, and whether Habit is its own item type) are left for #8.

### GTD

**Inbox**:
A Member's single collection point where captured things wait, still unclarified, until the Member decides what each one means.
_Avoid_: in-tray, in-basket, stuff list

**Clarify**:
Deciding what an Inbox item is and whether it is actionable. If it is, the Member decides its Next Action and whether it is a Project. Only a Member clarifies.
_Avoid_: process, triage, sort

**Organize**:
Putting a clarified item into the list, calendar or file where it belongs.
_Avoid_: file, categorise

**Reflect**:
Regularly looking over the whole system to keep it complete and current. Its keystone is the Weekly Review.
_Avoid_: review (on its own), check-in

**Engage**:
Choosing and doing a Next Action in the moment, filtering by Context, time, energy and then priority.
_Avoid_: do, execute

**Next Action**:
The next physical, visible step that moves a commitment forward. It is done once and then gone.
_Avoid_: task, to-do, step

**Project**:
A finishable outcome that takes more than one Next Action and can be done within about a year. It always has at least one open Next Action, Waiting For or Calendar entry.
_Avoid_: goal, initiative, epic

**Project Support**:
Notes and material that help with an active Project. It is not a reminder of anything to do.
_Avoid_: project notes, attachments, reference (for this)

**Waiting For**:
Something a Member is expecting from someone else, recorded with who owes it and since when.
_Avoid_: delegated, blocked, pending

**Someday/Maybe**:
Something a Member might do but hasn't committed to. It stays parked off the action lists until a review activates it or drops it.
_Avoid_: backlog, wishlist, later

**Reference**:
Information worth keeping that asks nothing of anyone.
_Avoid_: notes, archive, documents

**Calendar**:
The hard landscape: only what must happen on a particular day or at a particular time.
_Avoid_: schedule, due date list, planner

**Tickler**:
A reminder to bring something back to a Member's attention on a chosen future date, without committing to do it that day.
_Avoid_: snooze, follow-up, reminder

**Context**:
What a Next Action needs in order to be done (a place, a tool or a person). It is used to filter what can be done right now.
_Avoid_: tag, category, label, cue

**Agenda**:
A list of things one Member wants to raise with a specific person, usually the other Member, next time they meet.
_Avoid_: talking points, discuss list

**Checklist**:
A reusable list of steps or standards to run through when a recurring situation comes up. It is not a list of commitments.
_Avoid_: template, routine (until #8 decides)

**Weekly Review**:
A Member's weekly session to get clear (Inbox to zero, Mind Sweep), get current (every list, Project and Calendar entry) and get creative (Someday/Maybe).
_Avoid_: weekly check-in, retro, planning session

**Mind Sweep**:
A deliberate brain-dump of every open loop still in a Member's head into the Inbox, prompted by trigger lists.
_Avoid_: brain dump, braindump

**Horizons of Focus**:
The six levels of commitment from Ground (Calendar and Next Actions) through H1 Projects, H2 Areas of Focus, H3 Goals, H4 Vision, to H5 Purpose and Principles. Higher levels drive lower ones.
_Avoid_: altitudes, Runway/10,000 ft names, levels

**Area of Focus**:
An ongoing part of life a Member or the Household keeps up to a standard (Home, Health, Money, Family). It is never finished, and Projects come out of it.
_Avoid_: area of responsibility, category, life area

**Goal**:
An outcome wanted within about one to two years (Horizon 3). It sits above Areas of Focus and is reached through Projects and Habits.
_Avoid_: objective, target, OKR

**Two-Minute Action**:
A one-off action found while clarifying that takes under two minutes, so it is done on the spot instead of being tracked.
_Avoid_: two-minute rule (ambiguous), quick task

**Natural Planning**:
Thinking a Project through in five phases (purpose, outcome, brainstorm, organize, Next Actions) when listing the steps isn't enough.
_Avoid_: project planning, breakdown

### Atomic Habits

**Habit**:
A behaviour a Member repeats on a regular cue, meant to become automatic. It has no end state: success is that it keeps happening.
_Avoid_: routine, recurring task, chore (until #8 decides)

**Habit Loop**:
The four stages every Habit runs through: Cue, Craving, Response, Reward.
_Avoid_: habit cycle

**Cue**:
The trigger that starts a Habit: a time, a place, a preceding action, an emotional state or other people.
_Avoid_: trigger, reminder, context

**Craving**:
The desire for a change in state that a Cue produces.
_Avoid_: motivation, urge

**Response**:
The behaviour performed, which is the Habit itself.
_Avoid_: action, task

**Reward**:
The immediate payoff that satisfies the Craving and makes the Habit worth repeating.
_Avoid_: incentive, prize

**Four Laws**:
Clear's rules for building a Habit (make it obvious, attractive, easy, satisfying) and their inversions for breaking one (invisible, unattractive, difficult, unsatisfying).
_Avoid_: habit principles

**Identity**:
The kind of person (or Household) a Member wants to be. Each repetition of a Habit is a vote for it.
_Avoid_: persona, role, values

**System**:
The set of processes, mostly Habits, that produces results over time. It contrasts with a Goal, which is the result itself.
_Avoid_: process, workflow

**Implementation Intention**:
A Member's stated plan for a Habit: what they will do, when and where.
_Avoid_: schedule, plan, reminder

**Habit Stack**:
An Implementation Intention whose Cue is another Habit already in place: "after X, I will Y".
_Avoid_: chain, routine, sequence

**Environment Design**:
Arranging physical surroundings so a good Habit's Cue is obvious, or a bad habit's Cue is invisible.
_Avoid_: setup, priming

**Temptation Bundle**:
A pairing in which a Member only allows themselves something they want while doing a Habit they need to do.
_Avoid_: reward pairing

**Gateway Habit**:
The under-two-minutes starter version of a new Habit, used to master showing up before scaling up.
_Avoid_: two-minute rule (ambiguous), mini habit, starter task

**Habit Tracker**:
The record of each time a Habit was done, shown as an unbroken chain.
_Avoid_: log, streak counter, history

**Never Miss Twice**:
The rule that one miss of a Habit is forgivable but two in a row is not.
_Avoid_: streak rule, grace period

**Accountability Partner**:
The person who watches a Member's Habit so that skipping has a social cost. In the Household, the other Member.
_Avoid_: buddy, coach, watcher

**Habit Contract**:
A signed agreement between a Member and their Accountability Partner stating a Habit and the penalty for breaking it.
_Avoid_: pledge, commitment, deal

**Habits Scorecard**:
An inventory of a Member's current daily behaviours, each rated good, bad or neutral.
_Avoid_: audit, habit list

**Goldilocks Rule**:
The principle that a Habit stays motivating when it is just at the edge of the Member's current ability.
_Avoid_: difficulty tuning, sweet spot

**Plateau of Latent Potential**:
The stretch where a Habit is being repeated but results aren't visible yet, because the effort is building up.
_Avoid_: valley of disappointment (Clear's own phrase for how it *feels*; keep one name), stall

### Open (for #8)

- **Chore**: neither source defines it. Decide whether it is a recurring Next Action, a Habit, a Checklist, or a type of its own.
- **Routine**: Allen uses it for Checklists, Clear for habits or Habit Stacks. Decide whether Canoli needs it at all.
- **Recurring item**: GTD only holds repetition as Calendar or Tickler repeats. Decide how this relates to Habit.
- **Review**: the Weekly Review is defined above. Whether Habits are reviewed inside it, and whether Canoli adopts Clear's Annual Review or Integrity Report, is a design question.
