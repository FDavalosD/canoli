# What GTD prescribes, and which rules software can enforce

Research for [#2](https://github.com/FDavalosD/canoli/issues/2). Vocabulary (Household, Member, Proof, Escalation, Capture) follows [GLOSSARY.md](../GLOSSARY.md).

## Answer in one line

An app can mechanically enforce GTD's **structure and cadence**: things leave the inbox only once clarified, every project has a next action, every action has a context, the calendar holds only dated items, Waiting For items carry a person and a date, and reviews happen on schedule. It **cannot** enforce the **judgement** inside each step: whether something really is the next *physical* action, whether it really takes under two minutes, or whether a review actually involved thinking. For judgement rules the best an app can do is make them hard to skip (required fields, prompts, Proof, Escalation). It cannot check that they were done well.

## What GTD prescribes

### The five steps [S1 Ch. 2, S2]

1. **Capture**: "Collect what has your attention" [S2]. Every open loop goes into a small number of external in-trays, never kept in your head [S1 Ch. 1–2, 5].
2. **Clarify**: "Process what it means" [S2]. For each item, decide what it is and whether it is actionable [S1 Ch. 2, 6].
3. **Organize**: "Put it where it belongs" [S2]. Results go onto a defined set of lists and buckets [S1 Ch. 7].
4. **Reflect**: "Review frequently" [S2], chiefly through the Weekly Review [S1 Ch. 8, S4].
5. **Engage**: "Simply do" [S2], picking actions with the trusted system: first by context, then time available, then energy, then priority [S1 Ch. 9].

### The clarify decision tree [S1 Ch. 2 & 6, S3]

- **What is it?**
- **Is it actionable?**
  - **No**: **trash** it, **incubate** it (Someday/Maybe, or a tickler/calendar reminder to look again), or file it as **reference**.
  - **Yes**: **what's the next action?** The next physical, visible activity [S1, S6]. If the outcome needs more than one action, it is also a **project** and goes on the Projects list [S1 Ch. 2, S6].
    - **Less than two minutes?** Do it now [S1 Ch. 6, S9]. Allen calls two minutes "just a guideline" [S1 Ch. 6], and the rule applies when you are processing [S1 Ch. 6].
    - **Not yours to do?** **Delegate** it and track it on **Waiting For** [S1 Ch. 6].
    - **Otherwise, defer it**: to the **calendar** if it is tied to a day or time, or to a **Next Actions** list by context [S1 Ch. 6–7].
- Processing rules: take the top item first, handle one item at a time, and never put anything back into "in" [S1 Ch. 6].

### Definitions and organizing rules

- **Project**: any desired result that takes more than one action step [S1 Ch. 2; S6 "outcomes that require more than one action step"] and can be finished within about a year [S1 Ch. 2].
- **Every active project needs at least one next action.** You check this in the Weekly Review: "Review Project (and larger outcome) lists" and make sure each one has a current action [S1 Ch. 8, S4 step 8].
- **The calendar is the "hard landscape"**: day-specific actions, time-specific actions, and day-specific information only. Things you would like to do some day do not go on it [S1 Ch. 7].
- **Next Actions are sorted by context**, meaning the tool, place or person the action needs. Allen calls context the first criterion that limits your options, and suggests starting with Agendas, Anywhere, Calls, Computer, Errands, Home, Office and Waiting For [S7, S1 Ch. 7].
- **The core lists** [S1 Ch. 7, S6]: Projects; project support material; calendar; Next Actions (by context); Waiting For; Someday/Maybe; reference; a tickler (optional); checklists.
- **Agendas**: a list per person you deal with often, holding what to raise with them [S1 Ch. 7, S7]. In a household this means one Agenda per Member.

### Weekly Review [S4, S5, S1 Ch. 8]

The three phases are **Get Clear** (collect loose papers, get In to zero, empty your head), **Get Current** (review action lists, past calendar, upcoming calendar, Waiting For, Projects, relevant checklists) and **Get Creative** (review Someday/Maybe; "be creative & courageous") [S4]. The book recommends doing it weekly [S1 Ch. 8]. The 2009 post also says "Any time, any frequency, is better than not at all" [S4].

### Horizons of Focus [S8, S1 Ch. 2]

From the ground up: Ground (calendar and actions), H1 Projects, H2 Areas of Focus and Accountability, H3 Goals (1–2 years), H4 Vision (3–5 years), H5 Purpose and Principles [S8]. Higher horizons drive lower ones [S8]. The book asks you to review the lower horizons weekly and the higher ones less often [S1 Ch. 2, 8]. The official sources give no fixed cadence for H2–H5.

## Enforceability, rule by rule

**Fully** means the app can check the rule mechanically and block or flag a violation. **Partially** means it can force the step to happen, or require the fields, but cannot judge how well it was done. **Not** means the rule is about thinking that happens in the Member's head.

| # | GTD rule | Enforceable | What enforcement looks like (two-Member Household) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Capture everything into a small number of in-trays [S1, S2] | Partially | One GTD inbox per Member, plus the email agent and quick capture (share sheet, widget, Siri). The app makes the inbox the only way in: no item starts life as a project or action. It cannot make a Member capture what is in their head. |
| 2 | Capture doesn't decide meaning; clarify does [S1 Ch. 6; GLOSSARY "Capture"] | Fully | The email agent writes raw items only. It cannot set type, project, context or date. Only a Member's clarify action can. |
| 3 | Clarify one item at a time, top first, never back into "in" [S1 Ch. 6] | Fully | The clarify screen shows one item, oldest first, with no "skip" or "back to inbox" option. The only ways out are the decision-tree outcomes. |
| 4 | Every inbox item gets a decision: trash, someday, reference, or action [S1 Ch. 2, S3] | Fully | An item cannot leave the inbox without one of those outcomes. Inbox age is tracked: past N days, an item triggers nags, then Escalation to the other Member. |
| 5 | Actionable items get a next physical, visible action [S1, S6] | Partially | A required verb-first "next action" field, with a nudge if it reads like an outcome ("Plan…", "Organise…"). The app cannot verify that it is truly physical. |
| 6 | Multi-step outcomes become projects [S1 Ch. 2, S6] | Partially | When clarifying, the app asks "Is this more than one step?" and a yes creates the project together with its next action. Spotting multi-step items is the Member's call. The app can prompt when an action looks like an outcome or gets edited often. |
| 7 | **Every active project has at least one next action** [S1 Ch. 8, S4 step 8] | Fully | Hard rule: a project cannot be saved, or stay active, with no open next action, Waiting For or calendar item. Completing a project's last action forces "what's next, or is it done?" right away. A stalled project shows in the review and escalates. |
| 8 | Two-minute rule: do it now if it takes under 2 min [S1 Ch. 6, S9] | Partially | Clarify asks "Under two minutes?" A yes starts a 2-minute timer and the item has to be closed with Proof. The app can't know the real duration. It can flag items marked "<2 min" that then sit undone. |
| 9 | Delegate and track on Waiting For [S1 Ch. 6] | Fully (in-Household) / Partially (outside) | Delegating to the other Member creates their inbox item (or action) and a linked Waiting For for the delegator, with a required follow-up date. Completion with Proof closes both. For people outside the Household, the person and date are required and the follow-up date nags. |
| 10 | Calendar holds only the hard landscape [S1 Ch. 7] | Partially | A calendar item must have a date (and a time, or all-day), and the app asks "Must this happen on this day?" before saving. Whether it's a real deadline or a wish is the Member's call. The app can flag calendar items that keep getting moved. |
| 11 | Next actions are sorted by context [S1 Ch. 7, S7] | Fully | Context is required on every next action, from a Household context set (Home, Errands, Calls, Phone, Agenda:Chloe, Agenda:Fernando, Anywhere…). Location-based contexts can raise a reminder when you arrive (Errands near the shop, Home on arrival). |
| 12 | Agendas per person [S1 Ch. 7, S7] | Fully | Each Member has an Agenda list for the other. It surfaces when both are home, or at a shared review. |
| 13 | Non-actionable items go to trash, Someday/Maybe, or reference [S1 Ch. 2] | Fully (routing) / Not (judgement) | The outcomes exist and are required. Reference items can't have due dates. Someday items can't appear on action lists. Whether to keep something is the Member's choice. |
| 14 | Weekly Review happens [S1 Ch. 8, S4] | Fully (that it happens) | Scheduled per Member, or jointly for Household projects. Nags and Escalation follow if it's missed. The review is a guided checklist of the 11 steps [S4]; each step needs its own action before you can move on. |
| 15 | Review is thorough: In to zero, every project current [S4] | Partially | Proof of the review is mechanical: inbox at zero, every project's next action reviewed (touched or confirmed), every Waiting For with a past-due date dealt with, each Someday item seen. The app can't check the thinking. |
| 16 | "Empty your head" (mind sweep) [S4 step 3] | Partially | A timed capture step with Household trigger prompts (car, house, kids, money, health…). The step completes only with Proof, such as a minimum number of entries or an explicit "nothing new". The app can't know what's missing. |
| 17 | Get Creative: Someday/Maybe review, be courageous [S4 steps 10–11] | Not (beyond showing the list) | The app makes you go through the Someday list (each item: keep, activate or drop). The creative part is up to the Member. |
| 18 | Engage by context, then time, then energy, then priority [S1 Ch. 9] | Partially | A "Now" view filters by current context or location and a time-available input. Energy and priority are the Member's call. The app cannot force a choice. Due items fall under Proof and Escalation instead. |
| 19 | Horizons: projects serve Areas, Goals, Vision, Purpose [S8] | Partially (H2) / Not (H3–H5) | H2 is enforceable: every project must link to a Household Area of Focus (Home, Kitchen, Kids, Money, Health…), and an Area with no projects shows in review. H3–H5 can be stored and shown on a slower cadence (monthly, quarterly), but how they shape priorities is not checkable. |
| 20 | Trust the system: it must be complete and current [S1 Ch. 1, 8] | Not directly | Trust comes from rules 3, 4, 7, 14 and 15 holding. The app can show a "system health" score (inbox age, stalled projects, overdue Waiting For, days since review) to both Members. |

## Implications for the map

- **The strongest levers are invariants, not nags**: inbox items that must be clarified (rule 4), projects that must have a next action (rule 7), contexts that are required (rule 11), Waiting For items that must carry a date (rule 9). The data model can enforce these at save time.
- **Cadence rules (Weekly Review, inbox age, stalled projects) are where Proof and Escalation pay off.** They can be checked by machine (did it happen, is In at zero), but people skip them.
- **Judgement rules (rules 5, 6, 8, 10, 17, 18 and H3–H5) can only be prompted.** Forcing them harder would mean the app or the AI agent deciding meaning, which the GLOSSARY says Capture must never do.
- **Two-Member specifics**: delegation between Members (rule 9), Agendas (rule 12) and joint review of Household projects are GTD-native, and the app can track them precisely because both sides are in the system.

## Open questions (not answered by the sources)

- Whether Household projects have one owner or are shared. GTD assumes one person owns each project [S1], so this is a product decision.
- Review cadence for H2–H5: the official sources give none.
